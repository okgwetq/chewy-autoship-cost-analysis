## Chewy's own discounts, and exactly where each one stops

Chewy doesn't sell subscription tiers. It sells one thing with a set of attached discounts, and almost every complaint about "Chewy discounts not working" traces back to misunderstanding which discount applies to which order.

**Autoship is the main lever.** Chewy gives 5% to 35% off your first Autoship delivery, depending on the product, and 5% off on every subsequent delivery of the same item [2]. Three details matter more than the headline:

- The first-order discount is **capped at $20** [3]. A $300 first food order doesn't get $105 off — it gets $20. That cap makes Autoship far more attractive on a mid-sized basket than on a stock-up order.
- The 35% you see quoted is a one-time welcome rate. Anyone describing Autoship as "35% off forever" is quoting the first delivery.
- Autoship has no commitment. You can pause, skip, reschedule or cancel from your account at any time, and Chewy sends a reminder email roughly 3 to 5 days before each order processes [2].

There are also one-off offers layered alongside it. Chewy's own dog food listings currently advertise a new-customer promo — spend $49+, get a $25 eGift card — on product pages [4], and third-party trackers have documented a $20 version of the same new-customer offer in recent months [5]. Which one you get depends on the promo running when you check out, so look at the banner on the product page rather than assuming. Chewy also runs 20% off your first pharmacy order and $5 off your first order placed in the app [5].

Free shipping kicks in on orders over **$49** [2][5]. Remember that number, because it's the trap in the next section.

## Do the math on one real basket

Take two 40-lb bags of the True Acre grain-free chicken recipe — $107.98, or 80 lb of food.

- **Buying one-time:** $107.98, or $1.35/lb. Free shipping, since the basket clears $49.
- **First Autoship order:** 35% off would be $37.79, but the cap bites, so you get **$20 off** → $87.98. Then 7% cashback on the eligible order value, roughly $6, brings the effective cost to about **$82**, or roughly $1.02/lb. If the running new-customer eGift card offer applies to your account, that's another $25 of Chewy credit on a later order.
- **Every Autoship after that:** 5% off brings the basket to $102.58, and cashback at the current rate adds about $7 back → around **$95.40**, or roughly $1.19/lb.

Notice what changed. The first order is worth planning carefully; the recurring order is worth about 12% off in total, not 35%. That's still a meaningful gap over a year of feeding a large dog — but only if the Autoship cadence is set sensibly rather than left on the default.

Also notice the single-bag problem. One 40-lb True Acre bag costs $53.99. Apply a first-Autoship discount and you land at $35.09 — under the $49 free-shipping line. Discounts routinely push baskets below the shipping threshold, so check how Chewy calculates shipping on a discounted subtotal before you build a basket around a code.

## Setting the cadence so you don't over-order

Chewy's own guidance on Autoship frequency, by pet size, for a 30-lb bag of dry food: small dog (10–25 lb) every 6–8 weeks, medium dog (25–60 lb) every 4–5 weeks, large dog (60–100 lb) every 3–4 weeks, giant dog (100 lb+) every 2–3 weeks or a step up to a 40-lb bag [2]. For wet food, a medium dog eating a 24-can case daily is roughly a two-week cycle.

If you're not sure, set it too slow rather than too fast. Chewy's reminder email arrives 3 to 5 days before processing, which gives you a window to move the date forward without penalty [2] — the reverse, an over-full cupboard and a skipped delivery, is the more common complaint.

## When Chewy isn't the answer

Chewy's $1.35/lb entry point is competitive, but it isn't the cheapest route for everyone. Three cases where it loses:

- **Grocery-tier food.** On the very cheapest kibble tier, Walmart and Target shelf prices can undercut Chewy's Autoship pricing on the same item [2].
- **Niche brands Chewy doesn't stock.** If a brand isn't in the catalogue, no discount layer fixes that.
- **Prescription and pharmacy orders.** These are excluded from cashback entirely [6], so the cashback angle simply doesn't apply. The Autoship discount can still apply to eligible pharmacy items, but don't expect it to stack with cashback [2].

And if your dog is picky, resist putting a brand-new formula on Autoship for the first order. You'd be spending your one capped first-order discount on a food your dog may reject. Order it one-time, and put the winner on Autoship afterwards.

## Quick answers

**Does a promo code stack with Autoship?** Yes — a welcome code can sit on top of your first Autoship order, and that's usually the single biggest saving Chewy publishes [5]. But an unapproved code voids cashback on the entire order [6], so run the comparison before you paste anything in.

**Can I cancel Autoship after the discounted order?** Yes, from your account, with no cancellation fee [2]. Chewy's terms do look unfavourably on serial sign-up-and-cancel behaviour, so read them if that's your plan.

**Why is my cashback still pending?** ShopBack lists Chewy tracking at about 2 days and confirmation at 75 days [6]. If the order gets refunded, exchanged or returned, the cashback disappears with it [6].

**When are the best sale windows?** Chewy runs sales around Prime Day, Black Friday, Cyber Monday, Memorial Day, July 4 and New Year [2]. Those windows are when a site promo, Autoship and cashback all overlap — but the everyday Autoship-plus-cashback combination usually beats a one-off code for anyone buying food on a schedule.

The short version: pick the bag that prices well per pound, size the basket past $49, use your first Autoship discount on a food your dog already eats, and route the order through ShopBack so a percentage of it comes back. The 35% headline is a one-time thing. The 5% plus cashback is what actually shows up every month.
